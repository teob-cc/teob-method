# The ten steps — asks and deliverables

Each step states what it asks and what it produces. The written artifact is what the next step consumes.

---

## Phase I · Outside

### 01 · Outside
**Asks** Who calls this, at what volume, and what can they tolerate — and what is this organisation actually able to operate, host and afford?

**Demand.** Per client class: identity and origin (first-party app, partner system, internal service, machine, scheduled job) · trust zone (public internet, partner extranet, internal network, regulated enclave — and delegated authority, named now) · volume and shape (read/write mix, average and peak, burst profile) · tolerances — five, one line each; four recorded per class (latency percentile, ordering scoped to a key, durability, accuracy), the fifth per view, below · devices and networks · geography and regime · adversaries · your own dependencies, which hand you a quota rather than accept one.

**Every client-visible promise is declared here, and nowhere else.** Two of them are routinely deferred and must not be:

- **The views this class reads, each with its own freshness bound** — the fifth tolerance, and the one never recorded per class: one bound per view, not one per client. A buyer tolerates a five-minute-old recommendation and a two-second-old basket. Flattening those is how every read ends up on the strict store. The bound is the client's to state; step 03 decides only what it implies.
- **What this class is still handed when a dependency is down** — chosen from six client-visible outcomes in rising severity: correct but slower · correct but less · stale but complete · pending but signalled · absent but signalled · **wrong, which is never permitted.** Say which are accepted, how often, and what each costs them. Step 07 prices and proves these; it does not author them.

Every tolerance names the conditions it holds under. "p99 200 ms" and "p99 200 ms during the nightly partner window" are different requirements, and only the second is testable.

**Supply.** Team capability and size · hosting posture (cloud, on-prem, hybrid, sovereign-region) · contracts already held (CDN, archive tier, managed services) · cost envelope, as a ceiling and a unit · team topology (how many teams, owning what, on which release cadence — the boundary decision at 04 consumes it) · expected lifecycle (spike · one-off product · prototype becoming platform · platform) · compliance and procurement reality · where a model is in the graph, how its compute is sourced · **loss lines** beside the cost lines: what one unit of violation costs the business — an over-delivered impression nobody can bill, a refunded booking, an SLA credit, an hour of a paying class on rung 5; each a figure with provenance, usually assumed and so a risk-register entry · **consumers you cannot rebuild for** — who holds a client library, SDK, schema, image or public API version of this system that you cannot change and redeploy on their behalf. Name them. That one answer buys or refuses most of the delivery machinery at 04 and 09; repository splits still follow access and tooling constraints; rolling deploys, stored data and retained messages may still require compatibility.

**Stress the elicited list before leaving.** Take the nouns just written — each class, view, tolerance — and ask, no probability and the ridiculous allowed, *what hits this?* A discovered rung joins the ladder under the same signing rules; a mechanism worth designing but not building is a **recorded option** — designed, primed, unbuilt, trigger stated. Full pass at 07.

**Deliverable** A client-class table carrying quotas, tolerances, the view list with a freshness bound each, the accepted degraded outcomes and the shed order · a dependency list with the quotas and availability figures each grants you · an operating-envelope table, each row naming the later step it constrains, its loss lines beside its cost lines · any recorded options with their triggers. These are the **joint-signature artifacts**: everything the business half of the organisation can read, check and correct lives here, and nothing client-visible is decided after this step.

**Exit condition — no figure leaves this step unconsumed.** Beside each volume, name the later step that will divide, bound or decide with it. A figure with no named consumer is a question that should not have been asked: drop it, or find the decision it was standing in for. Checked again at 02, reported at 10.

### 02 · Load
**Asks** How much work arrives, how much data accumulates, and what may it cost to serve?

Four figures: peak request rate per client class (average and peak, with the peak factor stated) · data volume and growth rate over the horizon you are designing for · working-set size · cost envelope, carried in from the step-01 operating envelope rather than invented here. Amplification, node counts and replication overhead are deliberately absent — they are properties of a design that does not exist yet, and are computed in the sizing pass at 04.

**Divide per hot scope.** Divide each hot scope’s peak demand by the measured sustainable throughput of its actual enforcement protocol. 1 ÷ commit latency is only a one-at-a-time, stop-and-wait estimate; batching, pipelining and transaction shape can change it. Saturation rejects that implementation under those assumptions, not every serial protocol.

**Where a request fans out, the tail is a load figure too.** If the calls are independent and each of N parallel calls exceeds its p-th percentile with probability 1 − p, a request that waits for all N exceeds it with probability 1 − pᴺ: at p99, N = 10 gives about 10%, N = 100 about 63%. Holding the request at p99 across 100 leaves needs each leaf at about p99.99. Write the N and the leaf percentile it implies beside the step-01 latency tolerance; 07 chooses the answer.

**Deliverable** The four demand figures, shown as division, with every input labelled measured / contractual / assumed · the per-key ratio for every hot key · the fan-out tail wherever a request fans out. **No technology named yet.**

Every figure elicited at 01 is either divided here or explicitly carried to a named later step. One still unconsumed at 10 is a finding against 01, not against this step.

## Phase II · Shape

### 03 · Data
**Asks** What is true, what is derived from it, and what must never break?

Take the step-01 view list and its promised bounds, and inventory data classes, lineage, sensitivity, residency, temporal and custodial lifecycle, conflict policy, and replay/restore obligations. Inventory all irreducible inputs and their lineage before declaring anything rebuildable. Authority is independent of consistency: ownership, follows, policy and operation state can be truth without strict writes. View freshness, acknowledgement semantics and invariants determine coordination. A linearizable read does not reserve a resource; bind the promise to an atomic hold or conditional commit. A freshness promise needs a completeness watermark or equivalent evidence, an age check on the serving path, and a defined refusal, fallback or signed degraded bound when it cannot be met. An as-of marker and an alarm alone do not enforce the bound.

**The consistency menu is a ladder with a fork, and a second axis.** On order: linearizable → sequential → causal, and, hanging under causal, the four session guarantees side by side: read-your-writes, monotonic reads, monotonic writes, writes-follow-reads (Terry et al., 1994). The map has no arrow between the session guarantees. On age: eventual + a bound limits how old a read may be and says nothing about order, so only linearizable is above it — a causal or sequential store may serve a read of any age. **Sequential** means every reader sees the writes in one agreed order, though not necessarily the latest; it costs one ordering point (a leader or a totally ordered log) without a real-time read path; it fits coordination and configuration data read from followers. **Pick the weakest guarantee that satisfies the tolerance.** Where a class needs two that are not above one another — typically read-your-writes *and* monotonic reads — it needs both: the two mechanisms, or causal above them, whichever is cheaper, and the record says which. Causal is never bought by default as a way of getting two session guarantees at once.

**An invariant’s scope sets the proof obligation.** Record the actual isolation and check-and-change protocol. For one key, use an atomic conditional update where sufficient; across keys, serializable transactions or a proved locking/constraint protocol; across partitions, correct coordination or a changed signed promise. A saga is compensation, not proof of a bound.

**Place every invariant on the enforcement ladder.** E1 serialised, E2 escrowed, E3 bounded, E4 detected. Measure the chosen E1 protocol before ruling it out. Escrow is valid only for decomposable invariants with conserved rights, atomic local spending, fenced ownership and safe transfer/reclamation after failure. Rate × lag bounds require hard admission and lag limits, burst and in-flight allowances, and a defined response when those limits fail. Average rate and a lag alarm are not a hard bound; a saga alone is not E3. Price each permitted violation and get agreement on it; a zero-tolerance invariant admits no bounded violation.

**Deliverable** The step-01 view list with its authoritative inputs, projection lineage, consistency and freshness enforcement · an acknowledgement list with its kind per entry · an invariant list, each with its scope, the isolation that scope needs, the store's default isolation, its enforcement rung, and one enforcer per promise · the data-class table — truth or projection, consistency class, ordering key, lifecycle, size and growth. This is where most of the design is actually decided.

### 04 · Storage & transport
**Asks** Which store serves this access pattern at this size, and what exactly does the path between stores guarantee?

Enumerate surfaces first — one per client class × operation group, each with a contract, a sync or async response mode and idempotency on writes — before any store is discussed. Match store family to access pattern, and spend the operating envelope here, not at deployment: the capability, hosting posture and contracts recorded at 01 are consumed at this step. Geography is decided here too — replication topology (single-region, active-passive, active-active, per-region partitioning) changes what consistency you can honestly promise. Declare all **eight** channel properties per edge: sync or async — and if sync, whether the acknowledgement is commit-bearing · delivery guarantee · ordering key · backpressure · message TTL · payload contract (schema, versioning, compatibility mode — always answered; write *none* only when no old reader, retained message or persisted schema can cross the change) · **far side** · **departure**. Plus the inherited latency budget, which is sliced from the caller's and never invented locally. Then granularity: runtime units, module seams, repo topology — each split citing a force.

**Synchrony does not settle delivery semantics.** A timeout can leave the result unknown even when the operation is idempotent. Separate the operation’s durable state from the caller’s knowledge; expose a stable handle, status or reconciliation path, retry limits and an escalation deadline. Do not report refusal unless non-acceptance is known. Message business expiry is independent of the caller’s response deadline. Bound active connections, waiting requests, wait duration and overflow separately; a bounded pool can have unbounded waiters.

**Far side** — the far side of a channel is a set whose membership changes while the channel is open. Name the resolver (DNS, a registry, a mesh, a static list) and type it: a store, read through a projection, and a projection owes a staleness bound. That bound is how long this end goes on sending work to somebody who has left.

**Departure** — answered twice. Graceful is derived, not chosen: stop advertising, wait out the far-side bound, then finish what is in flight inside the budget the edge already inherits; drain for less and a planned departure is indistinguishable from a crash. Ungraceful is paid for by properties already chosen — an async consumer replays through its atomic effect/dedup protocol; an async producer loses what was buffered and unacknowledged; a synchronous caller is left unable to say whether its write happened.

**A brokered channel is specified more than once — and a store is brokered too.** Producing into a log and consuming from it are two channels around a store. So are writing to and reading from a database: the write path is a producer channel and each reader is a consumer channel of its own. On a log the asymmetry is retention (the store's) against the dedup key (the consumer's). On a database it is **consistency offered against consistency taken**. Name the database once and every one of those is a default.

**A store played by more than one process has channels inside it.** Postgres primary and replicas, a Cassandra ring, Kafka brokers under a controller quorum, a Pekko cluster holding sharded state: wherever two of its processes exchange data, the edge is a channel. Almost always two — the **replication channel** (each write from the process that took it to the ones that must hold it) and the **membership channel** (gossip, a consensus quorum, a controller: who else is there) — and a third where membership changes move data, the **rebalancing channel** (streaming, reassignment, shard handoff), which runs exactly when the store is weakest. Each owes the eight, answered by configuration lines whose defaults nobody chose: sync or async (synchronous replication is commit-bearing and spent from the writer's budget) · guarantee (what a replica that missed a write gets back) · ordering key (one log, per partition, or per key by timestamp — a conflict policy) · backpressure (the writer waits, drops the replica from the quorum, or never knows) · TTL (business expiry where applicable), plus a separate catch-up retention window (an unread slot can fill the disk) · contract (the format old and new members speak during a rolling upgrade) · far side (the membership list and its staleness) · departure (the rebalancing it sets off, the writes only it held). **The store's durability and its consistency offered are these channels seen from outside** — primary-only linearizable service may coexist with asynchronous replicas if reads, fencing and failover preserve the claim; lossless promotion requires the stated durable acknowledgement set. **Co-location is shared fate**: members sharing a host, rack or zone fail together, so write a placement rule beside the store — the failure domain no two members share, and quorum per domain (three members over two zones lose their majority with the zone holding two); placement executes it at 08.

**A clustered runnable unit is a split already taken.** Internal asynchronous transfers can require contracts even within one process; remote transfers always add latency and independent failure. A unit that runs as a cluster (Pekko cluster sharding, an in-memory grid, anything routing a message to the instance holding the entity) has channels between its instances — entity location is a store read through a projection, a message to a remote shard is a channel owing the eight, a member leaving sets off shard handoff. Count that cut as if you had made it, and cite the force that bought the cluster.

**The pairing rule.** Effectively-once effects require a defined operation key and an atomic effect, deduplication record and saved result, or a naturally idempotent conditional effect. Specify key scope, payload mismatch, concurrent retries, retention and replay after expiry. External effects need a downstream idempotency contract or reconciliation. Avoid independent dual writes: an outbox must commit with the authoritative mutation; CDC must retain the committed changes through recovery. A lease needs a clock assumption, fencing, transfer protocol and a failure response.

**Repo topology is a third axis.** Repository topology and consumer compatibility are separate decisions. A published SDK can live in a monorepo. Repo splits cite access, tooling, ownership or release constraints. Own mobile apps, rolling deployments, persisted data and queued messages can retain old versions; declare their compatibility windows even when every source file shares a commit. Size build tooling from measured build time against a stated tolerance; a remote cache requires hermetic inputs and a defined invalidation contract.

**The sizing pass — the second arithmetic pass, run here.** Capacity can only be computed after the design, because every term is a property of the graph just drawn; run it immediately, while the choice is still cheap to reverse. Write and read amplification · per-store rate (client rate × amplification × peak factor) · node count (required rate ÷ per-node capacity ÷ target utilisation — size for N−1, never for full) · stored bytes (logical volume × replication factor × overhead ÷ compression) · log retention (throughput × window) · partitions, and how the count changes later · cross-zone traffic · cost per request, checked against the envelope. Each split adds a hop to the amplification. **The reversal rule**: forty nodes to serve twelve transactions per second means the choice was wrong — return to the family selection; one node with headroom means delete the distribution and say why.

**The partition key is the scope key of the invariant that is hardest to move.** Where two invariants disagree about the key, one of them pays — guest-night against room-night in a booking system — and that is the finding; write down how the full invariant is coordinated, or which changed promise is explicitly accepted.

**Record the actual cost to reverse beside every decision.** Name the rollback window, data and keys that must survive, deployed consumers, migration work and external effects. A module seam is not a rollback guarantee. Before an expensive irreversible commitment based on an assumed figure, obtain the measurement or explicitly accept the uncertainty and consequence. Expand-and-contract preserves rollback only while old representations and readers remain usable; contraction can close that window.

**Record the ratio beside the citation.** Per choice: what the mechanism supplies over what the citing requirement demands, with the demand taken at its average so that the peak can be one of the factors you name. Near 1 is sized to the job; tens are headroom and must name the factor — growth, peak, failover; hundreds mean the requirement cannot discriminate the choice, and the choice came from somewhere other than this derivation. Then the fourth field, **what it introduces** — a cache a stale window and a stampede on expiry, a conditional write a per-key ceiling, hedging extra load on the hedged tier, two sources of truth a reconciliation to run — each item cited by the mechanism that answers it, carried onto the degradation ladder or the risk register, or accepted in writing. An introduced cost nobody records is how a design accretes mechanisms whose citations nobody can see.

**Deliverable** The labelled graph: every node typed, every edge annotated, the channels inside every store more than one process plays, each with its placement rule — plus a sizing table per store and channel, a cost-per-request figure and a stated headroom target · per decision, its ratio, what it introduces, and its cost to reverse.

**Compatibility:** validate every retained message/schema version that can replay, including rollback binaries; adjacent-version backward compatibility alone is insufficient. Deployment together does not erase persisted old data.

**Local execution:** transactions, locks and conditional writes supply atomicity. Process co-location alone supplies neither atomicity nor order; internal async transfers still need bounded queues and lifecycle contracts.

### 05 · Trust
**Asks** Who may see which data, under what authority, enforced where, provable to whom, and erasable how?

Enforcement — the trust zones came from 01 and the classification from 03. Identity propagation: the caller's identity travels across every hop; say whether downstream hops re-verify or trust an asserted context, and what stops the assertion being forged · **enforcement points**: per data class, the single surface that authorises access to it · the sensitivity × access matrix: per classification level, who may read, who may write, what is logged · tenancy and isolation: shared everything, row-level scoping, or store-per-tenant · encryption with a purpose — each layer names the adversary it defends against · the erasure mechanism: per-subject keys and crypto-shredding, and for custodial data the same machinery triggered by entity retirement, with a completeness report · audit as a data class of its own · residency as a mechanism that fails closed — and **a replica is a copy**: a replication channel into another region is an export, and the check fails closed on it too · **sending data to a model is an export**: the step-03 classification applies to every prompt and to the retrieved set, and model output is untrusted.

Authentication does not replace authorisation: state operation, object and tenant permissions even at a shallow boundary. Erasure inventories plaintext projections, caches, logs, exports, backups, replicas and key copies, with a deadline and verification per class. Crypto-erasure covers only ciphertext whose every usable key is destroyed; replay and restore must not resurrect erased data.

**Deliverable** A sensitivity × access matrix · a list of enforcement points · a tenancy model · an erasure procedure.

### 06 · Derivation
**Asks** For each derived data class: what computes it, from what input, with what state, and how is it reconstructed after loss or a bug?

Per processor: request-time / stateful stream / batch / model · inputs and outputs · state size and recovery time · **idempotent under replay, or it is not safe to operate** · keying and parallelism · late and out-of-order data — the lateness bound and the policy past it · the rebuild path: how long a full rebuild takes, and whether reads are served while it runs · merge semantics when combining across shards, hosts or windows · error bounds if it approximates — **signed**: where overshoot and undershoot have different prices (the step-01 loss lines), state the bound per side and choose which side the error falls on, toward the cheaper; escrow moves all of it to the undershoot · **the reconciler** for every E3 or E4 invariant — a processor with its own deadline (time to close), the enforcer of record for the exact figure and for anything billed · model processors (inference) sized like any other.

**Deliverable** A processor list — kind, input, output, state, recovery time, replay behaviour, rebuild duration, and the merge rule wherever partial results are combined · each approximation's bound per side · each reconciler with its time to close. Merge semantics are where correctness quietly dies — combining per-host top-K does not give global top-K.

## Phase III · Reality

### 07 · Failure
**Asks** For each rung promised at step 01: what has to be true internally to reach it, is it reachable on purpose and has it ever been exercised — and what does each edge do when the far side is gone?

1. Bring the promises forward from step 01 unchanged — the tolerances and the accepted degraded outcomes are already on the table. Do not re-ask.
2. **Price every rung both ways** — what reaching it costs to build, and what each occurrence costs the business, from the step-01 loss lines; the enforcement rungs from 03 the same. Set a mechanism's running cost against the expected loss it prevents: where the loss prevented is a small fraction of the cost, the mechanism is correct and unjustified — the verdict of a ratio in the hundreds.
3. Prove each promised rung is **reachable on purpose** — a flag, a breaker, a shed rule — and say when it was last exercised. A ladder never exercised is fiction. **Every degraded response carries a marker** — a partial flag, an as-of timestamp, a degraded header — so the caller can decide for itself; step 09 counts them. Where a model is in the path it has a ladder of its own — the full model · a smaller or quantised one · a cached or precomputed answer · the non-model path · pending-with-a-handle · absent-and-signalled — and every rung that changes the model is checked against the step-01 accuracy tolerance and still marked.
4. Mechanics per edge — timeouts sliced from the client's budget, a retry budget with backoff and jitter, idempotency on anything retried, circuit breakers, fallbacks, deadline propagation, dead-letter handling, and departure behaviour for both kinds of leaving. Blast radius: bulkheads per dependency, isolation per client class, cells so a failure takes a fraction of the users. Overload: shed at the edge in the step-01 shed order, return a clear signal, protect the core. Design for hostile retry behaviour — assume some callers retry immediately, without backoff, forever. Tail latency is arithmetic, and 02 did it: under the independent-leaf model, across N parallel leaves the request misses the leaf percentile with probability 1 − pᴺ (at p99, about 63% for N = 100 — Dean & Barroso, 2013). Pick deliberately: fewer leaves · **hedged requests** — a second request sent only after the first has been outstanding past about its p95 adds about 5% more requests under a stable leaf-latency distribution; bound extra work and overload feedback separately; immediate duplication is what doubles it · **partial results**, which is rung 2 and must have been promised at 01. Abuse and compromise are failure modes: for each, the blast radius, what detects it, and the containment action. Every mechanic traces to a rung a client asked for, or it goes.

**A fallback must preserve the accepted contract.** A timeout can leave the result unknown even when the operation is idempotent. Separate the operation’s durable state from the caller’s knowledge; expose a stable handle, status or reconciliation path, retry limits and an escalation deadline. Do not report refusal unless non-acceptance is known. On commit-bearing edges, a pending or unknown outcome needs its recovery path; an alternate write path must share the same idempotency and authority. Derive recovery objectives from actual durable acknowledgements, replication, fencing and promotion rules.

**Deliverable** Each rung, and each enforcement rung, priced both ways · a reachability proof per promised rung — what reaches it, and when it was last exercised · the per-edge failure spec · stated recovery objectives · for every store with replicas, the failover it runs (it loses exactly the writes the replication channel's guarantee allowed; who decides a member is gone — too fast is a split brain, too slow an outage) and the rung each class is served during it. The ladder is carried forward, not authored here.

**The stress pass** (from residuality theory — O'Reilly 2024, decomposed). After the ladder is priced: generate stressors by abusing the design's own nouns — every class, data class, schema, component — plus the business context. Rules: **no probability** · **everything goes in the list, however ridiculous** · **no generic stressor lists**. Then the incidence matrix (stressors × components, 1/0) read mechanically: high row = dangerous attractor · high column = a component absorbing capability classes it was never given · **two 1s in one row = hyperliminal coupling**, the co-failure the coupling count cannot see (the count reads the map at rest; the matrix reads it in motion) · a column whose rows are a subset of another's can live inside it · a zero column is under-stressed, not safe. **Three legal outputs per stressor**: a stated requirement (signed like any elicited one) · a finding · a recorded option. A stressor is a probe, never a requirement — it does not enter the map's first column and carries no number into the arithmetic. **Guard**: a matrix total is a measurement, not a target; a mechanism justified only by a stressor row is furniture with a scary story. Fires again at 10.

### 08 · Runtime & placement
**Asks** What can this team run well, what exactly do we ship, and where must it physically be?

Deliberately small — this step allocates machines and machinery, not semantics, and the sizing was done at 04 (with N−1 counting the rebalancing traffic the survivors carry, not a moment of rest). **Runtime by organisational constraint**: the capability recorded at 01, now spent — not preference, not novelty. **Polyglot has a price**: each additional runtime multiplies build, deployment, patching and on-call knowledge, so justify it by a genuine mismatch. **Placement executes the topology**: active-active or active-passive follows the replication decision at 04, regions and zones follow the residency rules, and sovereignty is enforced only for classes already labelled. Each replicated store's placement rule from 04 becomes concrete: which member sits in which failure domain, no two where one failure takes both — a scheduler left to its defaults packs replicas together.

**The artifact is decided here, one per runnable unit** — immutable, identified by content or commit, built once and promoted rather than rebuilt per environment. Delivery does not get to invent a runnable unit; if the pipeline emits a deployable the design never named, one of the two is wrong. **Each environment must name what it verifies that production cannot.** Environments are the most reliably uncited mechanism in a system: each is a full copy of the cost, the config drift, the data problem and the access-control surface.

**Deliverable** One sentence on runtime · the artifact and how it is identified, one per runnable unit · the environment list, each naming what it verifies that production cannot · a placement map of regions, zones and edges, annotated with which data classes may live in each and which members of each replicated store.

### 09 · Operations
**Asks** How do we know it is healthy, how badly is a client hurt when it isn't and which rung are we serving, how do we change it without an outage — and whose phone rings?

Detection proportioned to consequence — how badly a client is hurt, and where on the ladder that lands. Outcome KPIs in business terms (successful vs abandoned checkouts, not CPU). **Zero-tolerance counts**: promises with no acceptable rate, tracked as counts that must be zero. **SLOs** promoted directly from the step-01 tolerances, one per client class and operation. **Surface metrics**: rate, errors and duration per surface and per client class. **Channel metrics**: lag, queue depth, retry rate, dead-letter count — and for the channels inside a store, replication lag against the promised recovery point, members out of sync, membership changes. **Derived-data metrics**: staleness of every projection against its promised bound. **Rung metrics**: which rung each class is being served, and for how long — the rate of the step-07 markers, per class, is the rung distribution; a transition into a rung the class does not tolerate is a page. **Alerting**: pages fire on user-visible symptoms and error-budget burn, never on a bare metric, and every page names the client class being hurt; outcome KPIs get a change detector that opens an investigation, not a threshold page. **Audits**: for every "never wrong", a continuous check that does not depend on the system being healthy — reconciled out of band, alarmed at any non-zero. **Tracing** across every hop that carries a deadline. **Model operations**, where a model serves traffic: tokens in and out per client class, accelerator utilisation, time-to-first-token and inter-token latency, rung distribution across the model ladder, drift against the evaluation set. Release and delivery shape sized by lifecycle. Ownership: one owning team and pager per subgraph, matching the step-01 topology. **Security operations**: key rotation, access recertification, audit-log review with a named reviewer, secret and certificate expiry alarms, a rehearsed containment procedure. **Telemetry budget**: retention and cardinality per signal, priced — an unbounded label is a cost incident with a delay fuse. Cost attributed per data class and client class, checked against the step-01 envelope — and beside it the **loss** actually incurred: violations counted and priced by the step-01 loss lines, and for every E3 invariant its overshoot against the signed bound and its reconciler's time to close.

**Change is derived here too, and each mechanism cites something:**

- **Version scheme** ← who can decline an upgrade. Semantic versioning requires a declared public API, because the major number is a promise about that boundary. Calendar versioning answers *how old is this*, and fits upgrades driven by support windows, security horizons or regulatory dates. Where the consumer cannot decline, the version is **identity, not promise** — a content hash or commit id.
- **Rollout strategy** ← what must be observed before committing. **Canary** when a graded signal exists and is worth waiting for; its whole value is the abort metric, so a canary without one is a slow deploy at the same price. **Blue-green** when rollback must be *instant*, at the price of a second footprint and state migration solved twice. Rolling replace is the default and cites neither. Name the force, then the strategy.
- **Evolution** ← the change that must land without an outage. **Parallel change** (expand · migrate · contract) for an incompatible interface — *migrate* is the skipped phase, so give it an owner and a date. **Branch by abstraction** inside one runnable unit. **Strangler fig** for replacing a system that already exists.
- **Flags have four lifespans, and conflating them is why flag debt accrues.** *Release* toggles are transient and **their removal is part of the mechanism**. *Experiment* toggles live as long as the experiment. *Permissioning* toggles are an authorisation rule wearing a flag's clothes — enforce them at a surface. **Ops toggles are the degradation ladder's actuators**, and are long-lived by design: a rung nobody can step onto during an incident was a document, not a mechanism.
- **Desired state and drift** — inventory desired-state truth, pull channel, reconciler and running projection. A sole deployment writer does not remove partial apply, failed replacement or resource-loss drift. State detection, repair, authorised transition bounds and commit-to-live limits; a permanent controller needs a recorded obligation.
- **Delivery metrics** — the published set is currently **five**: change lead time · deployment frequency (throughput); change fail rate · deployment rework rate (stability); failed deployment recovery time. Read against a stated tolerance, not an industry percentile.

Configuration is a deploy with no build step and usually no review — versioned, diffable, revertible, alarmed on divergence. **Environment variables are the wrong home for the quasi-static class**: no history, no diff, no reviewer.

**Deliverable** The outcome-KPI list · an SLO table · rung metrics and audits derived from the step-01 ladder · a metrics and tracing list with a telemetry budget · a release procedure naming its rollout strategy, abort metric and last rehearsal · a version scheme naming the consumer it promises to · a flag register with a category and lifespan per entry · a migration approach · an ownership map with pager assignments · a cost attribution and the loss incurred, each checked against the step-01 envelope.

## Phase IV · Proof

### 10 · Trace
**Asks** Does every mechanism cite a requirement, every requirement map to a mechanism, and how many requirements name the *same* mechanism?

**Deliverable** The traceability map with a coupling count beside each mechanism · a short risk register ranked by consequence and exposure, with uncertainty, reversibility and shared dependencies recorded · a list of any figures left unconsumed · the stressor sheet with each row's disposition · a first experiment. Unmapped requirement → a gap; design for it. Unconsumed figure → a number collected at 01 that nothing here consumes; drop it, or name the decision it should have informed.

**A mechanism citing no constraint from outside — a client's, or a row of the operating envelope — takes the first disposition that applies — a search, not a menu.** ① **Delete** — nothing sits above it. ② **Surface a missing requirement** — tracing it lands on a constraint from outside that was always true and never written down; the common case, and often a *different* constraint from the one that bought the parent. ③ **Declare self-derived, naming the parent decision** — and the parent now faces the same test. Naming a parent moves the obligation up, it does not discharge it. Citations chain; the recursion ends at a constraint from outside — a client's, or a row of the operating envelope — or the finding sits at the root, and **a five-deep chain on an ungrounded adjective is one finding, not five.** Record the chain's root, its kind — demand, supply, self-derived or unrecorded — its depth and the link at which it stops tracing.

**Fire the stressors at the finished map — the audit's reverse direction.** The forward walk checks stated requirements hold; this checks the map under inputs nobody stated. Same three outputs, same guard. With a held-out set, report the residual index **with its denominator** — a direction signal, not a measurement.

**Count the column as a screening step.** A trace row records the requirement, mechanism, assumptions, argument, evidence and status (declared / argued / tested / violated). A planned test is not a passing test; a zero violation counter is not a correctness proof. Count shared citations to prompt failure-mode analysis, then rank risk by consequence, exposure, uncertainty and reversibility, not the count alone.

**The cutover, where something already exists.** In a running system an uncited mechanism is Chesterton's fence rather than a razor: report it as *no recorded reason — confirm before removing*, and produce an incremental path rather than a deletion. Named shapes: **strangler fig** across codebases, **branch by abstraction** inside one. Both keep the system releasable throughout, which is what makes them usable.

**The risk register** ranks consequence, exposure, evidence uncertainty and reversal cost. Shared dependencies prompt explicit failure-mode analysis. Every assumed input names the decision it supports and a falsifying experiment. For a one-way decision, buy the measurement before committing. The subtraction test asks what a budget cut removes and which promises would then need renegotiation.

**Re-run trigger.** Repeated violations trigger a diagnosis of requirements, protocol, implementation, observations and operating assumptions. Two failed fixes do not prove the truth model wrong. Re-run the earliest affected decisions and their dependency closure; widen the review when the root cause remains unknown.

## Who signs which artifact

The ten steps emit a contract and a set of working notes. Say which is which, or the business half argues about topology and the engineering half quietly moves a promise. The boundaries are not the step boundaries.

| Category | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| **The business supplies** | The operating envelope — capability, hosting posture, contracts held, cost envelope and its loss lines, team topology, lifecycle, the consumers you cannot rebuild for, compliance and procurement reality, and the compute source where a model is in the graph |
| **Both sign** | Client classes and quotas · every tolerance with its conditions · accepted degraded outcomes · zero-tolerance counts · **the shed order** · cost per request · retention and erasure commitments · published SLOs |
| **Engineering owns** | Primitive typing and the graph · store families · the eight channel properties · capacity arithmetic, ratios, headroom · derivation and merge semantics · runtime and placement |

The traceability map is joint **column by column**: *Requirement* both sign, *Mechanism* engineering owns, *Verified by* both sign again. The shed order looks like an implementation detail and is a commercial decision — whoever owns the revenue owns it.

## Amendment — the model is living, not re-derived

Constraints move. The model was not wrong: a store is a **role with constraints**, and a given database is one thing *sufficient* to play it. When constraints change, only the **sufficiency claim** expires. Name the claim that died; do not redo the derivation.

| What changed | Re-check | Explicitly untouched |
|---|---|---|
| New client class | Tolerances, quota, shed order, surfaces; check new data, history, lifecycle and invariants and all dependent decisions | Preserve other claims only after this dependency check |
| A tolerance tightens | The derivation producing that view; possibly the store under it; the alarm set at half the old bound | Client classes, invariants, the cost envelope |
| A volume moves 10× | Demand arithmetic and everything citing it — node counts, **partition counts**, recorded ratios, **per-key ratios and the enforcement rungs they decided** | Tolerances, invariants, acknowledgement semantics |
| An invariant is added | Enforcer, serialisation point for its contended unit, ack semantics on that write path; the isolation its scope needs, its enforcement rung, and the partition key if its scope key is harder to move | Everything not touching that invariant |
| Operating envelope shrinks | Store choices, placement, granularity, buy-vs-build; run the subtraction test for real | **Client tolerances** — they do not move because you got poorer; that is a finding to escalate with numbers |

**An amendment to an engineering-owned artifact needs no counter-signature. An amendment to a joint-signature artifact is void until signed again**, because it is a promise with a price. Any change that invalidates foundational assumptions may require broader re-derivation; no table row is an unconditional exclusion. Write the amendment where the original decision lives, with the date and the claim that expired.
