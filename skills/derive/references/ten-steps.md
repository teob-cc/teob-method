# The ten steps — asks and deliverables

Each step states what it asks and what it produces. The written artifact is what the next step consumes.

---

## Phase I · Outside

### 01 · Outside
**Asks** Who calls this, at what volume, and what can they tolerate — and what is this organisation actually able to operate, host and afford?

**Demand.** Per client class: identity and origin (first-party app, partner system, internal service, machine, scheduled job) · trust zone (public internet, partner extranet, internal network, regulated enclave — and delegated authority, named now) · volume and shape (read/write mix, average and peak, burst profile) · tolerances — five, one line each; four recorded per class (latency percentile, ordering scoped to a key, durability, accuracy), the fifth per view, below · devices and networks · geography and regime · adversaries · your own dependencies, which hand you a quota rather than accept one.

**Every client-visible promise is declared here, and nowhere else.** Two of them are routinely deferred and must not be:

- **The views this class reads, each with its own freshness bound** — the fifth tolerance, and the one never recorded per class: one bound per view, not one per client. A buyer tolerates a five-minute-old recommendation and a two-second-old basket. Flattening those is how every read ends up on the strict store. The bound is the client's to state; step 03 decides only what it implies.
- **What this class is still handed when a dependency is down** — chosen from six client-visible outcomes in rising severity: correct but slower · correct but less · stale but complete · pending but signalled · absent but signalled · **wrong, which is never permitted.** Say which are accepted, how often, and what each costs them. Step 07 prices and proves these; it does not author them.

Every tolerance names the conditions it holds under. "p99 200 ms" and "p99 200 ms during the nightly partner window" are different requirements, and only the second is testable.

**Supply.** Team capability and size · hosting posture (cloud, on-prem, hybrid, sovereign-region) · contracts already held (CDN, archive tier, managed services) · cost envelope, as a ceiling and a unit · team topology (how many teams, owning what, on which release cadence — the boundary decision at 04 consumes it) · expected lifecycle (spike · one-off product · prototype becoming platform · platform) · compliance and procurement reality · where a model is in the graph, how its compute is sourced · **consumers you cannot rebuild for** — who holds a client library, SDK, schema, image or public API version of this system that you cannot change and redeploy on their behalf. Name them. That one answer buys or refuses most of the delivery machinery at 04 and 09; where it is *nobody*, a second repository, a declinable version, a compatibility promise and a deprecation window are all uncited.

**Stress the elicited list before leaving.** Take the nouns just written — each class, view, tolerance — and ask, no probability and the ridiculous allowed, *what hits this?* A discovered rung joins the ladder under the same signing rules; a mechanism worth designing but not building is a **recorded option** — designed, primed, unbuilt, trigger stated. Full pass at 07.

**Deliverable** A client-class table carrying quotas, tolerances, the view list with a freshness bound each, the accepted degraded outcomes and the shed order · a dependency list with the quotas and availability figures each grants you · an operating-envelope table, each row naming the later step it constrains · any recorded options with their triggers. These are the **joint-signature artifacts**: everything the business half of the organisation can read, check and correct lives here, and nothing client-visible is decided after this step.

**Exit condition — no figure leaves this step unconsumed.** Beside each volume, name the later step that will divide, bound or decide with it. A figure with no named consumer is a question that should not have been asked: drop it, or find the decision it was standing in for. Checked again at 02, reported at 10.

### 02 · Load
**Asks** How much work arrives, how much data accumulates, and what may it cost to serve?

Four figures: peak request rate per client class (average and peak, with the peak factor stated) · data volume and growth rate over the horizon you are designing for · working-set size · cost envelope, carried in from the step-01 operating envelope rather than invented here. Amplification, node counts and replication overhead are deliberately absent — they are properties of a design that does not exist yet, and are computed in the sizing pass at 04.

**Deliverable** The four demand figures, shown as division, with every input labelled measured / contractual / assumed. **No technology named yet.**

Every figure elicited at 01 is either divided here or explicitly carried to a named later step. One still unconsumed at 10 is a finding against 01, not against this step.

## Phase II · Shape

### 03 · Data
**Asks** What is true, what is derived from it, and what must never break?

Take the step-01 view list and its bounds — they are promises and are not revised here. Resolve each into truth or projection: a view whose bound is looser than the write path is a projection. Then, per data class: which acknowledgement kind writes need (receipt / reservation / completion — and an acknowledgement the caller's own promise depends on makes that edge **commit-bearing** at 04) · invariants that no view can tell you about, each naming one authoritative enforcer and a scope · consistency class, chosen against the client tolerances · conflict policy wherever a class has more than one writer or accepts writes offline · read-your-writes seams · commitment reads (views a client commits against, which cannot be arbitrarily stale) · sensitivity classification, with the residency constraint and erasure obligation attached — decided here, enforced at 05 · minimisation: the cheapest privacy control is the field never collected · lifecycle to the end, in its two kinds — temporal (expires against a clock) and custodial (valid until the owning entity is retired, so the deletion trigger is an event, not a clock) · quasi-static reference data as a class of its own: usually truth rather than projection, versioned and revertible, with its own restore path.

**Deliverable** The step-01 view list resolved into truth or projection, each with its consistency class · an acknowledgement list with its kind per entry · an invariant list with one enforcer each · the data-class table — truth or projection, consistency class, ordering key, lifecycle, size and growth. This is where most of the design is actually decided.

### 04 · Storage & transport
**Asks** Which store serves this access pattern at this size, and what exactly does the path between stores guarantee?

Enumerate surfaces first — one per client class × operation group, each with a contract, a sync or async response mode and idempotency on writes — before any store is discussed. Match store family to access pattern, and spend the operating envelope here, not at deployment: the capability, hosting posture and contracts recorded at 01 are consumed at this step. Geography is decided here too — replication topology (single-region, active-passive, active-active, per-region partitioning) changes what consistency you can honestly promise. Declare all **eight** channel properties per edge: sync or async — and if sync, whether the acknowledgement is commit-bearing · delivery guarantee · ordering key · backpressure · message TTL · payload contract (schema, versioning, compatibility mode — always answered; where both ends deploy together *none* is a legal answer, written down) · **far side** · **departure**. Plus the inherited latency budget, which is sliced from the caller's and never invented locally. Then granularity: runtime units, module seams, repo topology — each split citing a force.

**Blocking answers two of the eight, not three.** On a synchronous edge the delivery guarantee collapses to *it returned or it did not* and message TTL is vacuous — write both down rather than skipping them. Backpressure does **not** go quiet: a bounded connection pool is a queue with a wait policy and an overflow behaviour, and unless it is bulkheaded per client class the batch job and the interactive path wait in it together.

**Far side** — the far side of a channel is a set whose membership changes while the channel is open. Name the resolver (DNS, a registry, a mesh, a static list) and type it: a store, read through a projection, and a projection owes a staleness bound. That bound is how long this end goes on sending work to somebody who has left.

**Departure** — answered twice. Graceful is derived, not chosen: stop advertising, wait out the far-side bound, then finish what is in flight inside the budget the edge already inherits; drain for less and a planned departure is indistinguishable from a crash. Ungraceful is paid for by properties already chosen — an async consumer replays from its commit point and the dedup key absorbs it; an async producer loses what was buffered and unacknowledged; a synchronous caller is left unable to say whether its write happened.

**A brokered channel is specified more than once — and a store is brokered too.** Producing into a log and consuming from it are two channels around a store. So are writing to and reading from a database: the write path is a producer channel and each reader is a consumer channel of its own. On a log the asymmetry is retention (the store's) against the dedup key (the consumer's). On a database it is **consistency offered against consistency taken**. Name the database once and every one of those is a default.

**A store played by more than one process has channels inside it.** Postgres primary and replicas, a Cassandra ring, Kafka brokers under a controller quorum, a Pekko cluster holding sharded state: wherever two of its processes exchange data, the edge is a channel. Almost always two — the **replication channel** (each write from the process that took it to the ones that must hold it) and the **membership channel** (gossip, a consensus quorum, a controller: who else is there) — and a third where membership changes move data, the **rebalancing channel** (streaming, reassignment, shard handoff), which runs exactly when the store is weakest. Each owes the eight, answered by configuration lines whose defaults nobody chose: sync or async (synchronous replication is commit-bearing and spent from the writer's budget) · guarantee (what a replica that missed a write gets back) · ordering key (one log, per partition, or per key by timestamp — a conflict policy) · backpressure (the writer waits, drops the replica from the quorum, or never knows) · TTL (how long the sender keeps what a lagging replica has not taken; a slot nobody reads fills the disk) · contract (the format old and new members speak during a rolling upgrade) · far side (the membership list and its staleness) · departure (the rebalancing it sets off, the writes only it held). **The store's durability and its consistency offered are these channels seen from outside** — a store declared linearizable over asynchronous replication carries two lines that cannot both be true. **Co-location is shared fate**: members sharing a host, rack or zone fail together, so write a placement rule beside the store — the failure domain no two members share, and quorum per domain (three members over two zones lose their majority with the zone holding two); placement executes it at 08.

**A clustered runnable unit is a split already taken.** "No channels inside a runnable unit" holds for one process, or for instances that share nothing. A unit that runs as a cluster (Pekko cluster sharding, an in-memory grid, anything routing a message to the instance holding the entity) has channels between its instances — entity location is a store read through a projection, a message to a remote shard is a channel owing the eight, a member leaving sets off shard handoff. Count that cut as if you had made it, and cite the force that bought the cluster.

**The pairing rule.** An at-least-once producer channel, plus a store that retains long enough to redeliver, plus a consumer channel with a stable deduplication key, equals effectively-once processing. Deduplication is a property of the consumer channel, never of the broker; an unqualified "exactly-once" names a product rather than the three places the guarantee is earned. **Avoid dual writes** — when two stores must agree, write one and derive the other: a transactional outbox, or change capture from the truth store. **Coordination is designed, not assumed** — wherever the graph needs exactly one of something, that is a lease: a store plus a channel with four extra declarations — what elects the holder, the lease duration against a named clock, the fencing token, and the rung handed to the client during a split brain.

**Repo topology is a third axis, independent of runtime units and module seams.** It is not a scale or team-count question: it is whether a caller can be reached by your commit. One repository until a caller exists that cannot — that is the *published interface* line, and it was answered at 01. Two teams who can still land one commit together have not bought a second repository; one customer-held SDK has. A build system beyond the ecosystem default cites a repository whose correct incremental set can no longer be computed from the directory layout — record the ratio, measured build time against a tolerance stated first. A remote build cache is **a derived store with an invalidation contract**: a non-hermetic build breaks the contract, and a broken one serves confident wrong answers with green dashboards.

**The sizing pass — the second arithmetic pass, run here.** Capacity can only be computed after the design, because every term is a property of the graph just drawn; run it immediately, while the choice is still cheap to reverse. Write and read amplification · per-store rate (client rate × amplification × peak factor) · node count (required rate ÷ per-node capacity ÷ target utilisation — size for N−1, never for full) · stored bytes (logical volume × replication factor × overhead ÷ compression) · log retention (throughput × window) · partitions, and how the count changes later · cross-zone traffic · cost per request, checked against the envelope. Each split adds a hop to the amplification. **The reversal rule**: forty nodes to serve twelve transactions per second means the choice was wrong — return to the family selection; one node with headroom means delete the distribution and say why.

**Record the ratio beside the citation.** Per choice: what the mechanism supplies over what the citing requirement demands, with the demand taken at its average so that the peak can be one of the factors you name. Near 1 is sized to the job; tens are headroom and must name the factor — growth, peak, failover; hundreds mean the requirement cannot discriminate the choice, and the choice came from somewhere other than this derivation.

**Deliverable** The labelled graph: every node typed, every edge annotated, the channels inside every store more than one process plays, each with its placement rule — plus a sizing table per store and channel, a cost-per-request figure and a stated headroom target.

### 05 · Trust
**Asks** Who may see which data, under what authority, enforced where, provable to whom, and erasable how?

Enforcement — the trust zones came from 01 and the classification from 03. Identity propagation: the caller's identity travels across every hop; say whether downstream hops re-verify or trust an asserted context, and what stops the assertion being forged · **enforcement points**: per data class, the single surface that authorises access to it · the sensitivity × access matrix: per classification level, who may read, who may write, what is logged · tenancy and isolation: shared everything, row-level scoping, or store-per-tenant · encryption with a purpose — each layer names the adversary it defends against · the erasure mechanism: per-subject keys and crypto-shredding, and for custodial data the same machinery triggered by entity retirement, with a completeness report · audit as a data class of its own · residency as a mechanism that fails closed — and **a replica is a copy**: a replication channel into another region is an export, and the check fails closed on it too · **sending data to a model is an export**: the step-03 classification applies to every prompt and to the retrieved set, and model output is untrusted.

**Deliverable** A sensitivity × access matrix · a list of enforcement points · a tenancy model · an erasure procedure.

### 06 · Derivation
**Asks** For each derived data class: what computes it, from what input, with what state, and how is it reconstructed after loss or a bug?

Per processor: request-time / stateful stream / batch / model · inputs and outputs · state size and recovery time · **idempotent under replay, or it is not safe to operate** · keying and parallelism · late and out-of-order data — the lateness bound and the policy past it · the rebuild path: how long a full rebuild takes, and whether reads are served while it runs · merge semantics when combining across shards, hosts or windows · error bounds if it approximates · model processors (inference) sized like any other.

**Deliverable** A processor list — kind, input, output, state, recovery time, replay behaviour, rebuild duration, and the merge rule wherever partial results are combined. Merge semantics are where correctness quietly dies — combining per-host top-K does not give global top-K.

## Phase III · Reality

### 07 · Failure
**Asks** For each rung promised at step 01: what has to be true internally to reach it, is it reachable on purpose and has it ever been exercised — and what does each edge do when the far side is gone?

1. Bring the promises forward from step 01 unchanged — the tolerances and the accepted degraded outcomes are already on the table. Do not re-ask.
2. Prove each promised rung is **reachable on purpose** — a flag, a breaker, a shed rule — and say when it was last exercised. A ladder never exercised is fiction. **Every degraded response carries a marker** — a partial flag, an as-of timestamp, a degraded header — so the caller can decide for itself; step 09 counts them. Where a model is in the path it has a ladder of its own — the full model · a smaller or quantised one · a cached or precomputed answer · the non-model path · pending-with-a-handle · absent-and-signalled — and every rung that changes the model is checked against the step-01 accuracy tolerance and still marked.
3. Mechanics per edge — timeouts sliced from the client's budget, a retry budget with backoff and jitter, idempotency on anything retried, circuit breakers, fallbacks, deadline propagation, dead-letter handling, and departure behaviour for both kinds of leaving. Blast radius: bulkheads per dependency, isolation per client class, cells so a failure takes a fraction of the users. Overload: shed at the edge in the step-01 shed order, return a clear signal, protect the core. Design for hostile retry behaviour — assume some callers retry immediately, without backoff, forever. Tail latency is arithmetic: fan out to N shards and the slow tail dominates, so pick hedged requests, partial results (a rung) or fewer shards deliberately. Abuse and compromise are failure modes: for each, the blast radius, what detects it, and the containment action. Every mechanic traces to a rung a client asked for, or it goes.

**A fallback is legal only where there is a lesser answer to drop into.** On a **commit-bearing** edge there is none: the fallback is either a lost write or a double one, and the reachable rungs are pending-with-a-handle or a typed refusal. **An ambiguous outcome is not a rung.** A timed-out synchronous write leaves the caller unable to say whether it committed — that is not *absent, signalled*, because nothing can honestly be signalled. Idempotency on anything retried is what collapses it back onto *correct, slower*; without it, it resolves into a duplicate, and a duplicate on a money path is *wrong*. **The drain interval is not set here** — it was derived at 04 from the far-side bound. **Recovery objectives are read off the earlier choices** — asynchronous replication means a non-zero recovery point; if recovery point and time cannot be read off the storage decisions, those decisions are incomplete.

**Deliverable** A reachability proof per promised rung — what reaches it, and when it was last exercised · the per-edge failure spec · stated recovery objectives · for every store with replicas, the failover it runs (it loses exactly the writes the replication channel's guarantee allowed; who decides a member is gone — too fast is a split brain, too slow an outage) and the rung each class is served during it. The ladder is carried forward, not authored here.

**The stress pass** (from residuality theory — O'Reilly 2024, decomposed). After the ladder is priced: generate stressors by abusing the design's own nouns — every class, data class, schema, component — plus the business context. Rules: **no probability** · **everything goes in the list, however ridiculous** · **no generic stressor lists**. Then the incidence matrix (stressors × components, 1/0) read mechanically: high row = dangerous attractor · high column = a component absorbing capability classes it was never given · **two 1s in one row = hyperliminal coupling**, the co-failure the coupling count cannot see (the count reads the map at rest; the matrix reads it in motion) · a column whose rows are a subset of another's can live inside it · a zero column is under-stressed, not safe. **Three legal outputs per stressor**: a stated requirement (signed like any elicited one) · a finding · a recorded option. A stressor is a probe, never a requirement — it does not enter the map's first column and carries no number into the arithmetic. **Guard**: a matrix total is a measurement, not a target; a mechanism justified only by a stressor row is furniture with a scary story. Fires again at 10.

### 08 · Runtime & placement
**Asks** What can this team run well, what exactly do we ship, and where must it physically be?

Deliberately small — this step allocates machines and machinery, not semantics, and the sizing was done at 04 (with N−1 counting the rebalancing traffic the survivors carry, not a moment of rest). **Runtime by organisational constraint**: the capability recorded at 01, now spent — not preference, not novelty. **Polyglot has a price**: each additional runtime multiplies build, deployment, patching and on-call knowledge, so justify it by a genuine mismatch. **Placement executes the topology**: active-active or active-passive follows the replication decision at 04, regions and zones follow the residency rules, and sovereignty is enforced only for classes already labelled. Each replicated store's placement rule from 04 becomes concrete: which member sits in which failure domain, no two where one failure takes both — a scheduler left to its defaults packs replicas together.

**The artifact is decided here, one per runnable unit** — immutable, identified by content or commit, built once and promoted rather than rebuilt per environment. Delivery does not get to invent a runnable unit; if the pipeline emits a deployable the design never named, one of the two is wrong. **Each environment must name what it verifies that production cannot.** Environments are the most reliably uncited mechanism in a system: each is a full copy of the cost, the config drift, the data problem and the access-control surface.

**Deliverable** One sentence on runtime · the artifact and how it is identified, one per runnable unit · the environment list, each naming what it verifies that production cannot · a placement map of regions, zones and edges, annotated with which data classes may live in each and which members of each replicated store.

### 09 · Operations
**Asks** How do we know it is healthy, how badly is a client hurt when it isn't and which rung are we serving, how do we change it without an outage — and whose phone rings?

Detection proportioned to consequence — how badly a client is hurt, and where on the ladder that lands. Outcome KPIs in business terms (successful vs abandoned checkouts, not CPU). **Zero-tolerance counts**: promises with no acceptable rate, tracked as counts that must be zero. **SLOs** promoted directly from the step-01 tolerances, one per client class and operation. **Surface metrics**: rate, errors and duration per surface and per client class. **Channel metrics**: lag, queue depth, retry rate, dead-letter count — and for the channels inside a store, replication lag against the promised recovery point, members out of sync, membership changes. **Derived-data metrics**: staleness of every projection against its promised bound. **Rung metrics**: which rung each class is being served, and for how long — the rate of the step-07 markers, per class, is the rung distribution; a transition into a rung the class does not tolerate is a page. **Alerting**: pages fire on user-visible symptoms and error-budget burn, never on a bare metric, and every page names the client class being hurt; outcome KPIs get a change detector that opens an investigation, not a threshold page. **Audits**: for every "never wrong", a continuous check that does not depend on the system being healthy — reconciled out of band, alarmed at any non-zero. **Tracing** across every hop that carries a deadline. **Model operations**, where a model serves traffic: tokens in and out per client class, accelerator utilisation, time-to-first-token and inter-token latency, rung distribution across the model ladder, drift against the evaluation set. Release and delivery shape sized by lifecycle. Ownership: one owning team and pager per subgraph, matching the step-01 topology. **Security operations**: key rotation, access recertification, audit-log review with a named reviewer, secret and certificate expiry alarms, a rehearsed containment procedure. **Telemetry budget**: retention and cardinality per signal, priced — an unbounded label is a cost incident with a delay fuse. Cost attributed per data class and client class, checked against the step-01 envelope.

**Change is derived here too, and each mechanism cites something:**

- **Version scheme** ← who can decline an upgrade. Semantic versioning requires a declared public API, because the major number is a promise about that boundary. Calendar versioning answers *how old is this*, and fits upgrades driven by support windows, security horizons or regulatory dates. Where the consumer cannot decline, the version is **identity, not promise** — a content hash or commit id.
- **Rollout strategy** ← what must be observed before committing. **Canary** when a graded signal exists and is worth waiting for; its whole value is the abort metric, so a canary without one is a slow deploy at the same price. **Blue-green** when rollback must be *instant*, at the price of a second footprint and state migration solved twice. Rolling replace is the default and cites neither. Name the force, then the strategy.
- **Evolution** ← the change that must land without an outage. **Parallel change** (expand · migrate · contract) for an incompatible interface — *migrate* is the skipped phase, so give it an owner and a date. **Branch by abstraction** inside one runnable unit. **Strangler fig** for replacing a system that already exists.
- **Flags have four lifespans, and conflating them is why flag debt accrues.** *Release* toggles are transient and **their removal is part of the mechanism**. *Experiment* toggles live as long as the experiment. *Permissioning* toggles are an authorisation rule wearing a flag's clothes — enforce them at a surface. **Ops toggles are the degradation ladder's actuators**, and are long-lived by design: a rung nobody can step onto during an incident was a document, not a mechanism.
- **Desired state and drift** ← a drift you must detect. Decompose before adopting — it is four primitives, not a tool: the desired-state repo is a **store, and it is truth** (quasi-static class, needs its own restore path, not rebuildable by replay); the agent's pull is a **channel** owing all eight properties, ordering key included; the reconciler is a **processor**; the running system is a **projection** owing a stated staleness bound, commit-to-live, alarmed at half. **Drift is a zero-tolerance count, not a dashboard.** Where nothing but the pipeline can write, there is no drift and the reconciler is uncited.
- **Delivery metrics** — the published set is currently **five**: change lead time · deployment frequency (throughput); change fail rate · deployment rework rate (stability); failed deployment recovery time. Read against a stated tolerance, not an industry percentile.

Configuration is a deploy with no build step and usually no review — versioned, diffable, revertible, alarmed on divergence. **Environment variables are the wrong home for the quasi-static class**: no history, no diff, no reviewer.

**Deliverable** The outcome-KPI list · an SLO table · rung metrics and audits derived from the step-01 ladder · a metrics and tracing list with a telemetry budget · a release procedure naming its rollout strategy, abort metric and last rehearsal · a version scheme naming the consumer it promises to · a flag register with a category and lifespan per entry · a migration approach · an ownership map with pager assignments · a cost attribution checked against the step-01 envelope.

## Phase IV · Proof

### 10 · Trace
**Asks** Does every mechanism cite a requirement, every requirement map to a mechanism, and how many requirements name the *same* mechanism?

**Deliverable** The traceability map with a coupling count beside each mechanism · a short risk register ranked by that count · a list of any figures left unconsumed · the stressor sheet with each row's disposition · a first experiment. Unmapped requirement → a gap; design for it. Unconsumed figure → a number collected at 01 that nothing here consumes; drop it, or name the decision it should have informed.

**A mechanism citing no constraint from outside — a client's, or a row of the operating envelope — takes the first disposition that applies — a search, not a menu.** ① **Delete** — nothing sits above it. ② **Surface a missing requirement** — tracing it lands on a constraint from outside that was always true and never written down; the common case, and often a *different* constraint from the one that bought the parent. ③ **Declare self-derived, naming the parent decision** — and the parent now faces the same test. Naming a parent moves the obligation up, it does not discharge it. Citations chain; the recursion ends at a constraint from outside — a client's, or a row of the operating envelope — or the finding sits at the root, and **a five-deep chain on an ungrounded adjective is one finding, not five.** Record the chain's root, its kind — demand, supply, self-derived or unrecorded — its depth and the link at which it stops tracing.

**Fire the stressors at the finished map — the audit's reverse direction.** The forward walk checks stated requirements hold; this checks the map under inputs nobody stated. Same three outputs, same guard. With a held-out set, report the residual index **with its denominator** — a direction signal, not a measurement.

**Count the column — the third reading.** Both invariants ask whether a cell is *filled*; neither asks how many requirements name the same mechanism. Above one is a **coupling finding**: those promises fail together. The instrument is a column count on the map you already have. It is **not** a defect to fix by splitting — a second store derived from a coupling number rather than a constraint outside the system is an uncited mechanism, and rule one deletes it. What the count buys: carry the mechanism onto the ladder as *n* entries rather than one (they may sit on different rungs); check whether the promises sharing it have the same signatories, because the strictest one now sets the floor for all of them; and rank the risk register by the count, since the highest is the largest blast radius on the page.

**The cutover, where something already exists.** In a running system an uncited mechanism is Chesterton's fence rather than a razor: report it as *no recorded reason — confirm before removing*, and produce an incremental path rather than a deletion. Named shapes: **strangler fig** across codebases, **branch by abstraction** inside one. Both keep the system releasable throughout, which is what makes them usable.

**The risk register** — the two or three edges most likely to be wrong, with what would falsify them, starting with every figure labelled assumed. **The first experiment** — what you would build first to retire the largest risk. **The subtraction test** — if the budget or the timeline halved, what comes out and what breaks? A design that cannot be cut down was never decomposed.

**Re-run trigger.** A zero-tolerance count that keeps firing after the mechanism has been fixed twice is an **attractor report**, not an ops problem — it is a signal to re-run the derivation from step 03, because the source of truth was modelled wrong.

## Who signs which artifact

The ten steps emit a contract and a set of working notes. Say which is which, or the business half argues about topology and the engineering half quietly moves a promise. The boundaries are not the step boundaries.

| Category | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| **The business supplies** | The operating envelope — capability, hosting posture, contracts held, cost envelope, team topology, lifecycle, the consumers you cannot rebuild for, compliance and procurement reality, and the compute source where a model is in the graph |
| **Both sign** | Client classes and quotas · every tolerance with its conditions · accepted degraded outcomes · zero-tolerance counts · **the shed order** · cost per request · retention and erasure commitments · published SLOs |
| **Engineering owns** | Primitive typing and the graph · store families · the eight channel properties · capacity arithmetic, ratios, headroom · derivation and merge semantics · runtime and placement |

The traceability map is joint **column by column**: *Requirement* both sign, *Mechanism* engineering owns, *Verified by* both sign again. The shed order looks like an implementation detail and is a commercial decision — whoever owns the revenue owns it.

## Amendment — the model is living, not re-derived

Constraints move. The model was not wrong: a store is a **role with constraints**, and a given database is one thing *sufficient* to play it. When constraints change, only the **sufficiency claim** expires. Name the claim that died; do not redo the derivation.

| What changed | Re-check | Explicitly untouched |
|---|---|---|
| New client class | Its tolerances, quota, shed-order position, the surfaces it touches; ladder gains a row | Data classes, invariants, the source of truth |
| A tolerance tightens | The derivation producing that view; possibly the store under it; the alarm set at half the old bound | Client classes, invariants, the cost envelope |
| A volume moves 10× | Demand arithmetic and everything citing it — node counts, **partition counts**, recorded ratios | Tolerances, invariants, acknowledgement semantics |
| An invariant is added | Enforcer, serialisation point for its contended unit, ack semantics on that write path | Everything not touching that invariant |

**An amendment to an engineering-owned artifact needs no counter-signature. An amendment to a joint-signature artifact is void until signed again**, because it is a promise with a price. The only change that buys a genuine re-derivation is the re-run trigger above. Write the amendment where the original decision lives, with the date and the claim that expired.
